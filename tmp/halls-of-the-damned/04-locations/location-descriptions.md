## Page 110

The Vallaki Blacksmith is a place of heat, noise, and controlled violence—steel shaped to survive in a
land that grinds everything else down. While Vallaki smiles in public and whispers in private, the forge
speaks plainly: iron endures, or it breaks.
Arrival — Read-Aloud Description
The rhythmic ring of hammer on anvil announces the blacksmith long before you see it. Smoke rolls
from a squat stone building, carrying the sharp scent of coal and hot metal. Sparks flare briefly through
the open doorway, then vanish into the gray air.
Inside, the forge burns hot and bright, casting long shadows across racks of tools and half-finished
blades. Weapons and horseshoes hang neatly along the walls, every item practical, repaired, and reused
more than once. The blacksmith barely looks up as you enter, hammer rising and falling with practiced
certainty, as if the world beyond the forge matters less than the steel beneath his hands.
The Forge at a Glance
Function: Weapon repair, toolmaking, horseshoes, armor maintenance	•
Structure: Stone-and-timber workshop with open forge	•
Lighting: Firelight and daylight; harsh shadows by night	•
Mood: Intense, pragmatic, honest	•
This is one of the few places in Vallaki where effort still produces results.

The Coffin Maker’s Shop sits in quiet resignation among Vallaki’s narrow streets—a business that
never lacks customers, only comfort. Where other shops close their shutters in fear, this one
remains open out of grim necessity.
Arrival — Read-Aloud Description
The smell hits you first—fresh-cut pine layered over old dust and something faintly sour beneath it.
The shopfront is narrow and unassuming, its windows clouded with sawdust and age. A simple
wooden sign hangs above the door, creaking softly in the wind.
Inside, unfinished coffins lean against the walls in various stages of completion. Wood shavings
blanket the floor, crunching softly underfoot. The light is dim, filtered through grime-streaked
windows, and every sound feels louder than it should. Somewhere in the back of the shop, a
floorboard creaks—then goes still.

## Page 117

The Town Square is the public heart of Vallaki—and its most effective instrument of control. Everything
meant to inspire obedience, fear, or forced joy eventually passes through here.
Arrival — Read-Aloud Description
The street opens suddenly into a wide, muddy square ringed by leaning timber buildings and narrow
balconies. Colorful banners and faded garlands hang overhead, snapping weakly in the wind, their
brightness at odds with the gray sky above. At the center of the square stands a massive wicker sun, its
straw body patched and repaired again and again, surrounded by scorched earth and trampled
footprints.
To one side, wooden stocks sit permanently assembled, the beams worn smooth by years of use.
Nearby, a simple gallows rises above the square—unused often, but never dismantled. Town guards
linger at every corner, spears in hand, eyes scanning faces rather than threats. Conversations here are
quiet, laughter measured, smiles practiced. No one lingers without purpose.
The Square at a Glance
Function: Festivals, announcements, punishments, executions	•
Terrain: Packed mud, trampled straw, scattered ash	•
Lighting: Natural light by day; torchlit and harsh by night	•
Mood: Oppressive, performative, watchful	•
The square is always prepared—as if punishment might begin at any moment.

## Page 120

The Residential District is where Vallaki truly lives—and where it quietly suffocates. Behind shuttered
windows and bolted doors, fear settles into routine. This is the district of whispered prayers, careful
smiles, and neighbors who know too much to feel safe.
Arrival — Read-Aloud Description
The streets narrow as you move deeper into the residential quarter, the buildings crowding close as if to
listen. Wooden homes lean inward, their upper stories nearly touching across the lane. Shuttered
windows line the walls like unblinking eyes, most of them closed even during the day. The air smells of
damp timber, smoke, and old meals stretched too thin.
Footsteps echo here—yours and others you never quite see. A guard turns a corner ahead, armor
creaking softly, then disappears again. Curtains shift. Doors close. Somewhere nearby, a child laughs
once before being sharply hushed. In this part of Vallaki, privacy is an illusion, and silence is survival.
District at a Glance
Function: Primary housing for Vallaki’s citizens	•
Terrain: Narrow dirt lanes, uneven cobbles, tight alleys	•
Lighting: Dim by day; lanterns and shuttered candlelight by night	•
Mood: Claustrophobic, anxious, quietly observant	•
The district feels lived-in—but never at ease.

## Page 123

The shore of Lake Zarovich lies along Vallaki’s northern edge, where the town thins and the land gives
way to cold water and drifting mist. It is one of the few places in Vallaki that feels less watched—not
because it is safe, but because it is inconvenient to control.
Arrival — Read-Aloud Description
The buildings fall away as you approach the lake, replaced by open ground and the smell of cold water
and rotting reeds. Lake Zarovich stretches out before you, dark and still, its surface broken only by slow
ripples and the creak of tethered fishing boats. Mist hangs low over the water, swallowing sound and
distance alike.
Rough wooden piers jut into the lake, their boards slick with moisture. Nets hang from posts to dry, stiff
and half-mended. A few small boats bob gently at their moorings, their hulls scarred by use and neglect.
The town feels far away here—muted, distant—though it never truly lets go.
The Shore at a Glance

Lighting: Open daylight by day; heavy fog and moonlight by night	•
Mood: Isolated, uneasy, deceptively calm	•

The Arasek Stockyard is Vallaki’s logistical backbone—a place of crates, ledgers, and locked doors. It
keeps the town supplied and connected to the outside world as much as Barovia allows, and for that
reason alone it attracts attention, suspicion, and secrets.
Arrival — Read-Aloud Description
The street widens into a fenced yard cluttered with wagons, stacked crates, and tarpaulin-covered
goods. A long timber warehouse squats at the far end, its doors reinforced with iron bands and heavy
locks. The air smells of damp wood, animal sweat, and old straw. Wheels are rutted deep into the mud,
evidence of steady traffic despite Vallaki’s isolation.
Workers move cautiously here, pausing to watch newcomers before returning to their tasks. Every crate
is marked, every wagon logged. Nothing leaves—or arrives—without being noticed.
The Stockyard at a Glance

St. Andral’s Orphanage sits in the long shadow of the church, a quiet refuge built from grief and
necessity. It is one of the few places in Vallaki where cruelty is not enforced—only endured. The children
here are not loud, not careless, and not innocent for long.
Arrival — Read-Aloud Description
The road narrows near the church, the noise of Vallaki fading into a subdued hush. A modest wooden
building stands just beyond the chapel grounds, its walls freshly patched but uneven, its roof weighed
down by age and weather. A small fenced yard surrounds it, empty save for a few worn toys and
footprints pressed deep into the mud.
Inside, the air smells of boiled vegetables, candle wax, and clean linens. Low voices echo softly down the
hall. Children watch from doorways and stairwells—curious, wary, and far too quiet. Somewhere deeper
in the building, someone hums an old Barovian lullaby, slow and careful, as if afraid to disturb something
fragile.
