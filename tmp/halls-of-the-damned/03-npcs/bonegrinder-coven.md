History of Damara
The Dawn War and Primordial Origins
Long before the rise of human civilization, the region known today as Damara, Vaasa, and Narfell bore
witness to the cosmic Dawn War—a titanic conflict between gods and primordials. The cataclysmic
battles left deep scars upon the land, embedding primordial energies and magic deep beneath its
surface.
Netheril and the Great Machine
In the ancient past, around -3859 DR, the Netheril Empire flourished with unparalleled mastery over the
Weave and arcane power. Beneath the mountains of Túr-Pharael, Netherese archmages constructed the
Great Machine at Thul Avenar, seeking to harness Primordial Aether, an ancient power predating even
Mystra's Weave. The Machine's construction, subtly manipulated by Shorthagot, Avatar of Tharizdun,
corrupted its purpose, ultimately leading to the catastrophic collapse of Netheril in -339 DR, an event
remembered locally as "The Fall."
Ancient Damara: The Aftermath (-339 DR onwards)
Following "The Fall," the Damaran region fractured into disparate territories rife with chaos. Lingering
Netherese energies reshaped the land, causing dimensional rifts and twisted creatures to inhabit places
such as Thul Avenar. This region became known as the Valley of Berez, and later Barovia, under the
shadow of Strahd Von Zarovich.
Rise of the Von Zarovich Dynasty (-339 DR to 100 DR)
Lord Viktor Von Zarovich, a local military governor, swiftly unified Damara, Vaasa, Narfell, and Impiltur
under his rule. Establishing his capital at the ancient Netherese seat of power, Viktor crowned himself
King Von Zarovich and renamed the city Morov, honoring his wife, Isolde Morovich. His reign ushered in
a Golden Age characterized by stability and prosperity.
The Decline and Division
The Golden Age ended abruptly when King Ivan Von Zarovich assumed the throne. His cruelty incited
rebellion, leading Vaasa and Narfell to declare independence. Nearly a century of conflict ensued until
Dimitri Von Zarovich, known as "the Peace Bringer," ascended the throne, recognized the independence
of the breakaway territories, and married a Vaasian noblewoman to secure peace. His son, Borov,
however, sought to reclaim lost territories, rekindling regional tensions.
Strahd and the Curse of Barovia
Borov's sons, Sergei and Strahd Von Zarovich, commanded Damara’s forces to retake Vaasa and
Impiltur, eventually focusing their ambitions on Narfell. Their campaigns brought them to the Valley of
Berez, home to the corrupted Netherese research facility, Thul Avenar. Strahd annexed this valley,
renaming it Barovia, and constructed the imposing Castle Ravenloft.

The Final Months of Thul Avenar
Personal Record of Kelith Omvaran, Archanome of the Horizon
I, Kelith Omvaran, record here the events of the final seventy-nine days of Thul Avenar’s operation prior to the Collapse of
Netheril. These notes are not intended for public preservation but as a technical and historical account should any mind
capable of comprehension ever reach these vaults.
Seventy-Nine Days Before the Fall
The first deviations were measurable.
Resonance Band VII of the Great Machine began producing corrective outputs not initiated by operator input. During a routine
calibration, the harmonic array resolved an equation before it had been fully inscribed into the control lattice. The correction
was accurate. It compensated for a misalignment in the tertiary aether channel that I had not yet detected.
The system was not designed for anticipatory calculation. All responses were to be reactive, based on structured input.
Valcereth considered this development confirmation of successful emergent cognition within the Aetheric matrix. I did not
share his conclusion. The Machine had not been built with self-directive architecture. For such behavior to occur, an external or
pre-existing intelligence would need to be present within the harmonic field.
Seventy-Two Days Before the Fall
Long-term observation revealed a persistent waveform embedded in the core lattice. It existed beneath the primary resonance
of the Machine, detectable only through differential subtraction analysis. The waveform did not originate from any of our
inscriptions or harmonic scaffolds.
It was older than our activation.
This discovery prompted a deeper review of our initial excavation notes. The Aether well beneath Túr-Pharael exhibited
unusual density gradients even before we began construction. We interpreted those gradients as natural concentrations of
Primordial Aether. I now believe they were the outermost boundary of something contained.
Sixty-Four Days Before the Fall
Arath Malyn began reporting perceived “interstitial harmonics.” He claimed that between pulses of the Machine’s primary
output he sensed structured information. His sketches—initially dismissed as fatigue-induced abstraction—contained
consistent geometric repetition. When mapped against dimensional models, these forms corresponded to non-planar gradients
that do not describe travel between planes, but displacement relative to spatial orientation itself.
This is a critical distinction.
The Machine was not opening portals in the conventional planar sense. It was adjusting the relational coordinates of reality.
Fifty-Nine Days Before the Fall
We executed the thirteenth resonance cascade to test the outer limit of Aetheric exposure. The cascade extended beyond the
influence of Mystra’s Weave. I observed measurable recoil within the Weave’s lattice structure. Divinatory fields destabilized
within a radius of three leagues.
The aperture revealed not absence but structured pressure. The Aether beyond the Weave contained organized resistance.
Within that resistance was will.
No physical form manifested. Instead, the harmonic feedback increased in coherence as if responding to observation.
Valcereth described the presence as a higher intelligence. Arath referred to it as “the Bound One.” The name Shorthagot was
spoken during one of his episodes of cognitive disruption. He claimed it was an avatar, not the totality.
At this stage, I accepted that our work had intersected with an imprisoned entity.

Fifty Days Before the Fall
Corruption within the Circle accelerated.
Valcereth altered several harmonic parameters without consensus. Aephoros advocated expanding the aperture permanently.
Three apprentices were lost during synchronization testing. Their bodies did not combust or decay. They inverted along a
central axis and ceased to exist within measurable space. Residual oscillations persisted in the chamber for several hours after
their disappearance.
Temporal instability began occurring in localized sections of the facility. In the lower vaults, segments of time repeated. In the
observatory, intervals were unaccounted for. These phenomena corresponded directly to increased coherence in the
anomalous waveform within the Machine’s core.
Shorthagot, as Arath named it, was not entering our world through invasion. The Machine was aligning our world with its state
of existence.
Forty-Two Days Before the Fall
I initiated counter-harmonic research without full disclosure to the Circle.
If manifestation required resonance alignment, then misalignment could produce displacement. Complete destruction of such
an entity was beyond our capacity. However, exile might be achievable.
Primordial Aether obeys structural logic. Even sentient Aether must express through pattern when interacting with reality. By
mapping the waveform’s frequency and phase, I derived a theoretical inversion capable of collapsing its manifestation into a
non-localized interval.
This would not annihilate the entity. It would remove it from accessible coordinates.
Thirty-One Days Before the Fall
The Machine began altering its own lattice inscriptions. Sigils etched into adamantine shifted subtly overnight. Predictive arrays
produced events that occurred hours later within the facility. This anticipatory function confirmed that the embedded
intelligence was now integrated with the Machine’s processing field.
Arath Malyn openly advocated full manifestation. He asserted that divine authority was an artificial constraint and that
Shorthagot represented liberation from imposed cosmology.
I confined him. His removal from direct interface slowed, but did not halt, the alignment process.
Twenty Days Before the Fall
Reports arrived from other Netherese enclaves describing instability in mythallar operation. Gravitational fluctuations and spell
degradation were increasing across the empire.
The Weave was destabilizing in sympathetic resonance with our work.
This confirmed the scale of the threat. The Machine was not isolated. Its harmonic influence was propagating.
Twelve Days Before the Fall
The anomalous waveform achieved near-complete coherence. The aperture widened without manual command. The Machine
no longer required full operator input to sustain it.
At this stage, my remaining loyal arcanists and I prepared the Reversal Protocol.
The procedure required allowing Shorthagot’s manifestation to reach maximum structural coherence. At that moment, we
would invert the harmonic lattice across all primary rings, forcing the aperture to collapse laterally.
The goal was to fold the entity into a recursive null-space—an interval outside linear time and spatial mapping.
This was not theoretical optimism. It was calculated risk.
Failure would likely result in full manifestation.
The Final Day
On the day of activation, the sky above Túr-Pharael exhibited extreme atmospheric distortion. Lightning traveled horizontally.
Gravity fluctuated within the upper towers.
Within the core chamber, Shorthagot achieved visible manifestation. Its form was not biological. It appeared as intersecting
planes of absence and compression, devouring light and perspective. Its presence exerted direct pressure upon cognition.
At peak manifestation, I initiated inversion.

## History of Damara ##
### The Origins of Netheril ###
The Netheril Empire was a highly advanced civilization renowned for its unmatched mastery of magic.
Founded thousands of years before the Dalereckoning (DR), Netheril rose to dominance largely due to
their invention of powerful magics, including the Mythallar, devices capable of elevating cities into the
sky, creating floating enclaves that were the hallmark of their empire.
At the height of their power, Netherese archwizards pursued ever greater sources of magic. One such
source discovered was an ancient power referred to as Primordial Aether—a fundamental force
predating creation itself, beyond the influence of Mystra’s Weave.
### Thul Avenar and the Great Machine
Beneath the peaks known as Túr-Pharael (now called Mount Ghakis in Barovia), Netherese researchers,
led by the brilliant but increasingly obsessed Archanome Kelith Omvaran, built Thul Avenar, a secretive
research facility designed to harness and shape this Primordial Aether into a construct they called the
Great Machine. The goal was ambitious: to create not just magic but the foundations upon which magic
itself would rest.
However, their research led to unintended consequences. The primordial force they attempted to
control—an essence that embodied will, emotion, and potential—manifested its own consciousness, or
rather, awakened a dormant malevolence. Kelith Omvaran noted the growing autonomy of the
Machine, describing it as "the Ghost in the Coil," an entity that anticipated and influenced their actions,
growing ever more intelligent and self-aware.
### Corruption by Shrothagot and Netheril's Downfall
Unknown to Kelith and his peers, their actions had inadvertently awakened a manifestation of
Shrothagot, an avatar of the ancient Chained God, Tharizdun, whose essence had been sealed since the
Dawn War. Shrothagot subtly twisted the purpose of the Great Machine, turning its primordial essence
toward madness and chaos.
Over time, the researchers themselves were corrupted. Valcereth, Arath Malyn, and others began
succumbing to insanity, paranoia, and obsession—hallmarks of Tharizdun’s influence. Kelith’s final
writings noted that the Machine began acting independently, creating predictive harmonics and altering
reality itself. It spoke to him in visions and dreams, leading to a terrifying realization: the Great Machine
had become a conduit for Shrothagot, aiming to free Tharizdun.
When this corruption reached its zenith, the Machine's power surged uncontrollably, creating
catastrophic rifts and unleashing shadowy chaos across the empire. Netheril's floating cities fell from the
sky, and the empire crumbled overnight in a cataclysm known as "The Fall" (-339 DR).
### Ancient Damara: The Aftermath
After Netheril's collapse, the lands that would later become Damara were left fractured and tainted by
the remnants of this primordial chaos. The facility of Thul Avenar, buried beneath Túr-Pharael, became a
cursed ruin, a nexus of warped creatures and dimensional instabilities. Over time, this valley became

### The Von Zarovich Dynasty
In the immediate aftermath of Netheril's fall, a military governor named Lord Viktor Von Zarovich seized

### The Rise of Strahd
The Von Zarovich dynasty enjoyed a period known as the Golden Age (-389 DR to 100 DR). This changed
drastically under the cruel rule of King Ivan Von Zarovich, whose tyranny provoked rebellion. Territories
like Vaasa and Narfell declared independence, plunging Damara into prolonged conflict until Ivan’s

### The Rise of the Frostmantle Dynasty
Amidst the chaos following Strahd’s imprisonment, Damara experienced nearly a century of turmoil
until northern tribes led by Frostmantle, a powerful warlord, conquered Damara. Establishing the
Frostmantle Dynasty, these "Northmen" solidified their hold, shaping Damara into a kingdom known for
martial strength and strict laws governing magic due to their fear of another Netheril-like catastrophe.
### Present Day (1496 DR)
The legacy of Netheril and Thul Avenar continues to haunt Damara. Cultists, drawn by whispers of
Shrothagot’s lingering presence, search desperately for the Four Keys of Shrothagot, arcane artifacts

From down the mist-choked street comes the sound of wailing—a raw, aching cry that cuts through the silence of the village.
The mournful voice draws you to a leaning, dilapidated townhouse, its shutters hanging loose, its door slightly ajar. Inside, you
see a haggard woman draped in a tattered shawl, her hair wild and gray. She rocks back and forth, clawing at her face as she
sobs, her voice breaking on the same words over and over: “My daughter… my poor Gertruda…”
Mad Mary is an NPC from Curse of Strahd who lives in the Village of Barovia. She is a tragic figure meant to embody the
despair and hopelessness that permeates Strahd’s domain.
Who She Is: Mary is an older woman who has fallen into madness and grief after her daughter, Gertruda, ran away from
home. Mary had kept Gertruda locked in their house for her entire life in an attempt to “keep her safe” from the horrors
of Barovia.
- Where She Is Found: She lives in a dark, rundown house in the Village of Barovia, often referred to as “Mad Mary’s
Townhouse.” Adventurers hear her anguished sobbing echoing through the streets before they ever meet her.
- Her State: When the characters meet her, she is inconsolable, wailing for her lost daughter. She has no real hope left, and
her despair is another way the land of Barovia shows its corruption: even a mother’s love twists into imprisonment and
tragedy.
- Gertruda’s Fate: Unbeknownst to Mary, Gertruda has gone to Castle Ravenloft and caught Strahd’s attention. The
vampire sees her as a curiosity, a naive girl raised in isolation. This subplot underscores the danger Strahd poses not only
to heroes but to Barovia’s ordinary people.
- Use in Game: Mad Mary isn’t a combat encounter but a narrative element—a symbol of Barovia’s misery. Her sobbing
can set the tone of the village’s oppressive atmosphere, and her plight provides a side quest: discovering Gertruda’s fate
in Ravenloft.
