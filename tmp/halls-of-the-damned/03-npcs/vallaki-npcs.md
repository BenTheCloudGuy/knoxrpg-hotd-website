## Page 134

Wachterhaus is one of Vallaki’s oldest residences—an ancestral home steeped in resentment, decay, and ambition. Where the Burgomaster’s mansion projects forced order and
brittle optimism, Wachterhaus offers something different - Think Old Money.. The Wachter family comes from a long line of Cultist who practice peagan arts of Demon Worship
from Narfell, long before Strahd and the mists came. Fiona's husband was killed by Werewolves over a decade before, he was a historian, and was killed while travelling to some
ruins in the south, around the base of Mount Ghakis. Nobody knows who murdered him. But the Vistani found and bought his bodyhome (what was left of it.).
Fiona in honor of her husband continued to collect artifacts, books, anything on the history of the region for his Library at the Wachterhaus. It is one of the largest collections
outside of Castle Ravenloft.. And even Strahd has come to view the library a few times. Nikolai Wachter built the library over the course of decades and it was his pride. He treated
with Strahd and the two discussed ancient histories and lost languages together over wine from the Wizards of Wine vineyard. It was from Strahd that Nikolai learned of the
ancient Netheril ruins at Mount Ghakis.
Wachterhaus is one of the oldest houses in Vallaki, and it feels like it.
The Burgomaster’s mansion tries too hard. It wants to look orderly. Cheerful. In control. Wachterhaus does not. It has the look of old money and old blood. Wealth that no longer
needs to impress anyone. The place is full of age, dust, resentment, and the quiet certainty that the family inside it believes it belongs above everyone else.
That history runs deep.
The Wachters are not merely old Barovian nobility. Their line reaches back to older cult blood from Narfell, long before Strahd, long before the Mists closed around the valley. With
them came old practices—pagan rites, demon worship, forbidden symbols, and household traditions that survived because they were buried under respectability and inheritance.
None of it is displayed openly. It is hidden where families like this always hide such things: in heirlooms, marginal notes, coded records, and books no priest was meant to see.
And the heart of it all is the library.
This was Nikolai Wachter’s work.
He built it over decades. Not as a hobby, and not as decoration, but as the labor of a serious historian. He gathered books, maps, copied chronicles, old family records, travel
accounts, linguistic notes, religious fragments, and anything else tied to the deep history of Barovia and the lands around it. By the time he died, it had become one of the greatest
private collections in the valley outside Castle Ravenloft itself.
Strahd knew it. More than that, he respected it.
He came to Wachterhaus more than once to see the collection. Nikolai and Strahd spoke together about old histories and lost languages, drinking wine from the Wizard of Wines
while discussing matters few men in Barovia could have followed. That was how Nikolai first learned of the ancient Netherese ruins near Mount Ghakis. The knowledge came from
Strahd himself.
And once Nikolai had that thread, he pulled on it.
More and more of the later records in the library circle back to the south—to old stone near the base of Mount Ghakis, to rumors of buried structures, to shepherds’ tales, Vistani
accounts, ruined foundations, broken carvings, and strange objects brought down from the mountain by scavengers, hunters, and fools. The sources do not all agree, but together
they point in one direction: there is something old buried there. Older than Barovia. Older than Strahd’s war. Old enough that the name Netheril appears in the notes with the sort
of caution men use when they know a word matters but do not fully understand why.
Thorian finds references to artifacts as well. Bits of worked stone. Black metal that did not corrode. Fragments marked with symbols no local scholar could read. Objects that felt
wrong in the hand. Pieces men sold, hid, prayed over, or threw into rivers after a night or two in their homes. Most of the reports are secondhand. A few are not.

Then comes the darker part.
Over a decade ago, Nikolai traveled south to investigate those ruins for himself. He never came back alive.
The common story is that werewolves killed him. That may even be true. But nobody really knows who murdered him. By the time the Vistani found him, there was not much left
to bring home. They returned what remained of his body to Wachterhaus, and that was the end of Nikolai Wachter.
Or it should have been.
Instead, Fiona kept his work alive.
In his memory, she continued to expand the library. She bought books, relics, notes, copied manuscripts, and any record tied to the old history of the valley. What Nikolai began as
scholarship, Fiona preserved as legacy. She made sure his collection endured, and in doing so she turned Wachterhaus into one of the richest storehouses of forbidden history in
Vallaki.
That is where Thorian finds the piece that changes everything.
Buried among Nikolai’s notes, cross-referenced with older Wachter records and one anonymous copied account, is a reference to Tatyana leading Strahd to ruins near Mount
Ghakis. Not alone. Seraphine Stormraven was with them.
It is not written like a folktale. It is written like a preserved fact.
If that account is true, then Strahd did not learn of those ruins by accident, and he did not come to them alone. Tatyana brought him there. Seraphine stood with them. Whatever
waited in those stones mattered enough to be remembered in a house like this, by men like Nikolai, and discussed by Strahd himself over wine and old books.
That makes Mount Ghakis more than a ruin site.
It ties together Nikolai’s death, the Wachter library, Strahd’s private scholarship, strange artifacts brought out of the south, and a moment from the past when Tatyana herself led
Strahd into something ancient and buried.
And if Nikolai died chasing that truth, there is every chance someone wanted it buried for good.
