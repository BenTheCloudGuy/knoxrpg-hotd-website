Timeline of Damara (From Fall of Netheril to Current Time)
-389 DR: Fall of Netheril
The Great Collapse: The magical catastrophe leads to the fall of Netheril, throwing the region,
including what will become Damara, into chaos. The breakdown of Netherese order leaves a
power vacuum, sparking regional conflicts and the rise of local warlords.
- -300 DR: Rise of Damara
Emergence of Clan Von Zarovich: Amid chaos, the Von Zarovich clan consolidates power,
controlling vast territories, including Damara, Vaasa, Narfell, and Impiltur. They bring a semblance
of order, albeit under a stern rule.
- -200 DR: Consolidation and Stability
Rule of Barov I: Barov Von Zarovich expands and stabilizes the region through political alliances,
military conquest, and trade.
- -100 DR: Era of Knowledge
Lord Vladimir Von Zarovich: Emphasis shifts from conquest to scholarship. Great academies and
magical institutions are founded, bringing prosperity and stability.
- 1 DR: Dale Reckoning Begins
Damara prospers under stable governance. Settlements grow into influential city-states, bolstered
by trade routes and knowledge transfer from southern regions.
- 300 DR: Age of Conflict
Lord Ivan Von Zarovich: His reign is marked by internal strife. Bloody rebellions occur, brutally
suppressed by Ivan’s military might.
- 600 DR: Golden Age of Culture
Lord Dimitri Von Zarovich: Cultural renaissance blossoms. Damara sees monumental works of
architecture, art, and learning.
- 900 DR: Darkening Borders
Lord Gavriel Von Zarovich: Supernatural threats rise again. Gavriel founds knightly orders to
battle emerging evils from the Shadowfell and the Underdark.
- 1000 DR: Militarization and Expansion
Lord Barov II Von Zarovich: A stern and warlike leader, Barov II leads Damara into numerous
aggressive campaigns. Damara’s borders expand significantly.
- 1090 DR: Birth of Strahd
Strahd Von Zarovich is born to Lord Barov II and Queen Ravenovia Van Roeyen, destined to inherit
a powerful, warlike state.

## Page 227

In the tenth year since the Mists rose and clasped this valley in their pale fist, I
commit these thoughts to parchment, though my heart misgives me for doing
so. I write from the halls of Argynvostholt, where I have sought brief shelter
among men of honor, yet the matter of this writing lies far to the south in the
teeth of Mount Ghakis.
Much has been spoken in low voice concerning the Count—how he fell into
darkness, how blood and grief unmade him. These tales are told by hearthlight
and cloaked in pity or in fear. I have no use for such stories. I have seen
another root. Some weeks past, I journeyed southward into the peaks with two
companions, men sworn to vigilance and silence.
The mountain paths were narrow and the wind carried a sound not unlike
grinding stone. We climbed until the air itself seemed thinned of mercy. With me
went Aurelian Kael Thorn of the Tower, a wizard of keen mind and stern
discipline, and Sir Godfrey, Master of Arms to Lord Argynvostholt, whose oath
binds him as surely as iron binds a gate. We three went seeking knowledge of
that power which gave unnatural birth to the Count’s damnation and sealed this
valley from the wider world, in hope that what was done by craft might yet be
undone by craft.
The stones are cut in the Netherese fashion—severe, exacting, bearing marks
of geometries I have seen only in fragments of proscribed texts kept under seal
within our Order. Circles within circles, channels carved not for water nor
drainage but for the passage of force. Aurelian walked those lines with grave
silence and named them akin to workings attributed in forbidden glosses to the
Great Machine, a construct of elder age said to draw upon primordial aether
and anchor reality itself as a smith anchors iron upon anvil. There is in that
place a dais of black stone, smooth as night-water, and about it are etched
chains descending into an emptiness no mason dared to render. The air within
that ruin presses upon the chest. Even Sir Godfrey spoke softly there. The snow
does not lie long upon its threshold, though the wind is cruel. We found upon
the inner stones faint inscriptions—scraped, as though some hand had sought
to erase them. One word remained legible: Shadarlogth.
