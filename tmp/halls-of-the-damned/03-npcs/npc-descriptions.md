The Account of Marko Vaduva, Master Builder to Count Strahd von Zarovich
If you want the truth of it, I can give you only what I saw. I was there when Castle Ravenloft was built. I drew the plans. I
walked the mountain before the first stone was cut. I chose where the walls would stand, where the towers would rise,
where the lower vaults would bite into the rock. The count wanted a fortress that would last and a seat worthy of his name.
I gave him both. In those days I thought well of Strahd.
Men speak of him now as if he had always been a monster. That is easy talk. Most of them never knew the man. I did. He
was hard. He was proud. He was not warm, and he was not easy to serve. But he was a great lord. He had won the valley by
force of arms. He understood war, stone, supplies, distance, weather, walls, and men. When he looked over my plans, he
did not ask foolish questions. He saw what mattered at once. Could a gate hold? Could a tower command the road? Could
men live through a winter siege? He understood those things better than most nobles understand their own table.
The castle was his mother’s name made stone. That much is true. Ravenloft. He ordered it so. Whatever else came later, he
meant to honor her when he named it. I remember the work well. The great hall took longer than he liked because the span
of the roof was difficult and the mountain winds were worse than my first estimates. The chapel went up cleaner. Good
proportions there. Strong ribs. Clear sound. A man speaking at the altar could be heard from the rear without shouting. The
walls along the outer court were thick enough to stop rams and high enough to make climbers regret they had ever been
born. The lower levels were another matter. Cold stone. Storage. Crypts. Water channels. Service ways. Prison cells. I knew
every step of it.
There was one entrance below that worried me from the start. Not a proper gate. Not a postern for men-at-arms. A foul
thing. A runoff and sewer tunnel cut into the lower rock where the filth and stormwater could drain away down the
mountain. Narrow. Wet. Easy to miss from a distance, but still a way in if a desperate man found it. It led, by twists and a
hard climb, toward the lower dungeons. At first I had it secured with a single steel gate. Heavy enough, I thought. Locked
from within. Bars set deep into the stone. Elandra Stormraven disagreed.
She was the eldest of the three sisters. She knew war, and she trusted very little that had not yet failed under pressure. She
walked the walls with me more than once. She asked good questions. Better questions than many captains. She wanted to
know where the mortar might crack in winter, where the cisterns could be poisoned, where a climbing line might be thrown
from the dark without being seen. When I showed her the lower passage, she studied it a long while and said the gate was
not enough.
So I improved it. I narrowed the bend before the gate. I put a false section in the floor where a man stepping wrong would
break through rotten grating and drop into foul water below. I set iron teeth beyond the threshold where a rushing man
would not see them in the dark. Further in, I rigged a spring trap in the dry stretch before the dungeon stair. Elandra
wanted intruders hurt before they ever reached a proper door. I gave her that. If some fool came through the waste tunnel,
he would not pass it cleanly.
The Stormravens had served the von Zarovich line for years, and those three sisters stood above the rest of their house.
Elandra was the soldier. Seraphine knew dark studies and battlefield magic. Kaelin moved like a shadow and had the
patience to wait where other killers twitched and gave themselves away. Their loyalty to Strahd was complete. No one in
that court doubted it, and they wouldn't dare put it to the test.
Then Sergei came to Ravenloft. He was not like his brother. That is not criticism. It is only fact. Sergei was younger, easier in
manner, and loved without effort. Servants smiled when he passed. People spoke more freely near him. Strahd commanded
a room. Sergei put it at ease. Shortly after that Tatyana came, and after that nothing was ever right again.

been shot by more than a dozen crossbow bolts. Wounded. Surrounded. It should have been done. Instead he endured, and
then he fell on those around him with a fury I will not try to describe at length.
Then the sky went black. I do not mean clouds rolled in. I mean the day itself seemed to fail. The mists rose up around
Ravenloft and around the whole valley. It came thick and wrong and fast. Men at the outer wall vanished waist-deep in it.
Then shoulder-deep. Horses in the lower court screamed and could not be calmed. Torches guttered. Some said the
mountain itself had begun to breathe. Others fell to their knees as Strahd and those loyal to him killed everyone. By dawn,
no living soul remained in Ravenloft save him and his loyalist. The castle and the valley were taken by the mists, and Barovia
was no longer a place that belonged to the world the way other lands do.
As for the Stormraven sisters, they stayed with him even then. There may have been a moment of hesitation. I believe there
was. Even the most loyal hound can smell blood and know the master has changed. But they did not leave him. Elandra
knelt to the old oath. Seraphine saw power in what he had become. Kaelin, I am told, tried to flee and was hunted down in
the castle depths. In the end they all belonged to him. They followed him into victory, and then into damnation. People
speak of Castle Ravenloft now as if it were always a cursed place. It was not.
It was a noble house built to endure. It had a strong keep, good cisterns, service passages hidden where they ought to be
hidden, upper halls made to impress envoys, crypts beneath for the family dead, and lower dungeons meant for those who
had earned chains. It had faults, as any great work does. Too much height in some of the older towers. Too much
dependence on mountain weather for concealment. That sewer entrance below which I never liked. But it was sound. It
was my best work. Then Strahd damned it.
Do not misunderstand me. Stone is still stone. The great hall still stands where I placed it. The chapel still bears weight
where I told the masons it would. The lower passage is still there, unless the count himself has altered it further. If some
band of fools ever goes hunting through the waste tunnel below the cliff, they will still find the steel gate I set there, or
what remains of it, and if Elandra’s traps have been kept in order they may yet lose blood before they ever see a dungeon
wall.
But the castle changed when the mist came. Or perhaps it only became more truly a part of its master. I do not know which.
A hall can feel longer than it should. A stair can seem steeper in the dark than by daylight. A room can keep a cold that no
hearth drives out. Ravenloft became that kind of place.
So if you ask me what happened to Strahd, this is my answer. He was a great man who could not bear losing what he
thought should be his.

The Thul sites were never public works. They were imperial black laboratories, scattered far from Netheril’s heart so that no rival archmage, no priesthood, and no foreign court could see the whole design. One

Great Machine.

Entry A
I am the Ancient. I am the Land. My beginnings are lost in the darkness of the past. I was the warrior, I
was good and just. I thundered across the land like the wrath of a just god, but the war years and the
killing years wore down my soul as the wind wears stone into sand.
All goodness slipped from my life. I found my youth and strength gone, and all I had left was death. My
army settled in the valley of Barovia and took power over the people in the name of a just god, but with
none of a god’s grace or justice.
I called for my family, long unseated from their ancient thrones, and brought them here to settle in the
castle Ravenloft. They came with a younger brother of mine, Sergei. He was handsome and youthful. I
hated him for both.
From the families of the valley, one spirit shone above all others. A rare beauty, who was called Tatyana,
and I longed for her to be mine. I loved her with all my heart. I loved her for her youth. I loved her for
her joy. But she spurned me. Old One was my name to her — elder and brother also. Her heart went to
Sergei. They were betrothed. The date was set.
With words she called me brother, but when I looked into her eyes they reflected another name —
death. It was the death of the aged that she saw in me. She loved her youth and enjoyed it. But I had
squandered mine. The death she saw in me turned her from me. And so I came to hate death — my
death.

Blinsky Toys is a splash of color in a town that has forgotten how to enjoy it. Bright paint, whirring
mechanisms, and cheerful designs clash violently with Vallaki’s fear-soaked streets—making the shop
feel less like a refuge and more like a fragile act of defiance.
Arrival — Read-Aloud Description
A riot of color greets you before the door even opens. The shopfront is painted in reds, blues, and
yellows so bright they seem almost aggressive against Vallaki’s gray streets. A wooden sign swings
overhead, carved with smiling toys and cheerful letters that feel slightly too wide, slightly too sharp.
Inside, shelves overflow with dolls, wind-up animals, painted soldiers, and wooden puzzles. Many smile.
Some grin. Others stare with glassy eyes that seem to follow you as you move. The smell of fresh paint
and sawdust hangs in the air, undercut by something faintly chemical. Somewhere in the shop, a toy
clicks and whirs to life—then stops.

Donavich reveals that Doru was bitten during the battle — by one of his chosen champions:
Seraphine Stormraven.

Since then, Doru has been trapped in torment — not fully turned, but corrupted, trapped between
man and monster.
- “I have prayed night and day, for if Seraphine is destroyed, perhaps the curse may be lifted… and
my boy restored.”
4. Strahd’s Reach & Omniscience
Donavich warns that Strahd sees all through the eyes of his servants.	•
Wolves, bats, even villagers — all may be his spies.	•
Strahd relishes breaking hope before crushing it completely.	•
5. Ireena Kolyana
Donavich confirms Strahd’s obsession with Ireena, noting her uncanny resemblance to Tatyana.	•
He urges the adventurers to protect her above all else.	•
6. The Villages of Barovia
Village of Barovia: The people are cowed and hopeless. Many fear Donavich is mad because of his
constant prayers.
- Vallaki: A fortified town westward, more secure, but ruled by a man Donavich calls “a fool who
hides behind false festivals.”
- Krezk & the Abbey: Once a holy place, now twisted by some darker power.	•
7. Relics of the Morninglord
Donavich speaks of ancient relics of the Morninglord: a sacred symbol and a blade of sunlight said
to still exist in Barovia.
- Only such power, he believes, can truly banish Strahd’s evil.	•
Tone & Delivery
Donavich weeps with relief at the players’ mercy, apologizing for his earlier desperation.	•
His words falter between confession and prayer, muttering “Morninglord guide us…” as he shares
his tale.
- The revelation about Mordenkainen, Doru, and Seraphine Stormraven should land as a heavy
foreshadowing hook — proof Strahd is not invincible, and that the Stormravens’ corruption may
yet be undone.

Has the ability to turn those who have been bitten. But must destroy/cure the Vampire that bit them.

## Page 371

Gertruda..
A young woman draped in a silken gown steps lightly into view, her wide eyes drinking in the world with
innocent wonder. Her long, dark hair falls in loose waves, and a jeweled necklace glitters at her throat—
gifts far finer than anything she could have brought from the village. Yet there’s a strangeness in her
gaze: a childlike curiosity untempered by caution, as though she has never known fear. She smiles
faintly, almost dreamily, like someone who cannot grasp the danger she walks beside.
Angle 1: The Enchanted Guest (Oblivious & Enthralled)
Gertruda is dazzled by the castle, the food, and the flattery of Strahd. She has no sense of the danger.
Behavior: She giggles at the smallest things, marvels at the “wonderful feast,” and clings to
Strahd’s every word like a child listening to a bedtime story.
- Roleplay Notes:
Compliments the players’ clothes and manners, as though they’re attending a gala instead
of a vampire’s lair.
  - Defends Strahd if the players speak ill of him: “He’s been so kind to me—so much kinder
than my poor mother, who kept me locked away like a bird in a cage.”
  - Displays no fear, even when the atmosphere turns threatening. She truly believes she is
living a fairy tale.
  - - Angle 2: The Frightened Prize (Realizing the Horror)
Gertruda is beginning to see the cracks in the illusion. The lavish feast cannot hide the chill of the castle,
nor Strahd’s true nature.
Behavior: She eats little, glancing nervously between Strahd and the players. Her voice trembles
when she speaks, though she tries to smile.
- Roleplay Notes:
May whisper to the players when Strahd is distracted: “Please… don’t leave me here.”	○
Avoids Strahd’s touch or flinches subtly when he grows too close.	○
Conflicted: she resents her mother’s imprisonment, yet is beginning to wish she had never
left home.
  - If pushed, she admits: “I thought this was a dream… but now I see it’s a nightmare.”	○
