“They called it discovery. I called it temptation given form.”
I was there when they first spoke of it—the Great Machine. A device unlike any mythallar or
conduit ever conceived. No longer would we rely on the fickle weave of the gods, nor beg the
favor of Mystra. No. They wanted something... new. A font of power untouched by divine law or
natural limit.
The Arcanists of the High Table—led by Archmages Veltheran, Kel’Dazur, and the elusive
Aephoros the Boundless—believed they had pierced a curtain of reality that had never been
drawn back before. They claimed it was not simply a spell, nor a school, but an entirely new form
of magic—a kind of Primordial Engine, capable of rewriting the rules of existence itself.
To build such a thing, they needed distance from the prying eyes of rival enclaves and the divine
agents of Mystra who still watched the skies. They chose a site deep in the northern highlands,
beside the icy waters of the Tur-Pharael—the River of Ghosts. There, they raised a new citadel of
black crystal and silver spires.
They called it Thul Avenar, the “Gate of the Horizon.”
I recorded every design, every ritualized forging of its crystalline heart. The Machine grew beneath
our feet like a slumbering god—its gears forged from adamantine etched with living runes, its core
encased in spellglass that shimmered with all colors at once and none at all. They spoke often of
“harmonics,” “interdimensional sequencing,” and “the resonant field of thought itself.”
To them, I was a mere scribe. But I saw what they could not: a hunger beneath the magic. A
presence. When the Machine was silent, it still listened. When it moved, it did so with a rhythm
not written by mortal hand.
And then... they activated it.
What followed, I cannot say. My records from that day are torn, both from parchment and from
my mind. I remember only a great pulse, like the tolling of a celestial bell. Light and shadow
collapsed into one. Time trembled.
When I awoke, Thul Avenar was silent. The Machine—dark. Its heart, still beating with that
infernal rhythm, but muted. Something had been loosed, or perhaps bound, within. The Arcanists
were... changed. Or absent. I fled. I buried what I could. And I wrote this, the last truth I dared
preserve.
Let it be known: the Great Machine was not meant for this world.
Its name is lost in their pride. But I call it now by what it has become...
The Infernal Machine.

was.
Core Identity
Archmage Vael-Toruun was one of Netheril’s greatest arcane engineers and one of the most trusted
master-architects of its later age. Publicly, he was known as a brilliant builder of magical systems,
defensive latticeworks, containment engines, and power-distribution networks. Privately, he was
Shorthagot in disguise, embedded deep inside Netheril’s highest circles to guide its greatest minds
toward the creation of the Infernal Machine.
When Netheril fell, Vael-Toruun was believed dead along with countless other archmages. The truth was
buried. He was not destroyed. He was sealed within the Great Machine he helped create, and the
knowledge of that prison was hidden by the first Order of the Yellow Rose. Four sacred keys were set
apart to keep the prison closed, and the order was founded to guard that secret until the end of ages.
What the world knew him for
Vael-Toruun was not just tied to one site. He oversaw multiple state-level arcane projects across
Netheril. His name was attached to works involving:

Here’s a cleaner version in that frame:
Mythallars were the well. They held and stabilized immense arcane power. But that power was not
always drawn from the source directly. The Netherese learned to channel it through smaller crystal
matrices—what later ages would call Ioun Stones. These stones served as taps into the greater reservoir,
letting Netheril feed controlled magical power into engines, wards, weapons, and other arcane works
without needing a full mythallar at every site.
Many of Netheril’s greatest inventions relied on this principle. Rather than building every wonder
around a mythallar itself, they built systems that could draw from one through these tuned crystal
conduits. In later ages, the original purpose of many such stones was forgotten, and they came to be
known only as rare floating relics of strange magical power.
At the heart of every automaton was one of these mythallar crystals. What people now call an Ioun
Stone was once the core regulator, power tap, and arcane logic center of countless Netherese
constructs. The art of shaping and attuning mythallar crystals was one of Netheril’s highest secrets, and
it was lost when the empire fell. What remains are fragments, broken theories, and a handful of
surviving stones whose true purpose is no longer understood.
Here’s a slightly more in-world version if you want it to read like campaign lore:
The mythallar was the well. The crystals were the taps.
That was the foundation of Netherese arcane engineering. A mythallar held vast magical force in stable
form, while smaller tuned crystals drew measured power from it and carried that energy into the
empire’s great works. These crystals powered wards, engines, levitating structures, weapons, and
constructs. What later ages call Ioun Stones were, in many cases, the surviving remnants of that older
science.
Every true automaton carried such a crystal at its heart. It was more than a battery. It regulated power,
held arcane instructions, and linked the construct to the greater magical systems around it. The
Netherese called these cores mythallar crystals. The knowledge required to make them died with
Netheril, and now the world mistakes their surviving creations for relics, curiosities, or singular magic
items, never realizing they were once pieces of a much larger design.
