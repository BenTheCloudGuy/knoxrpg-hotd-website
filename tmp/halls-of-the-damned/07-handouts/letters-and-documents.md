## Page 63

To His Majesty, King Barov Von Zarovich,
My Father and Liege,
From the shrouded heights of Barovia’s north, I send this missive with tidings both wondrous and
enigmatic.
Sergei and I have discovered ruins in the wild reaches the local tribes call Solovid’Otchayaniye—
translated from their tongue as the Night of Despair. The name is no idle superstition. There is
power in this place. Old, bitter, and ancient.
The structures we unearthed are unquestionably Netherese in origin. The stonework, the arcane
resonance in the air—it is unmistakable. I believe we stand atop the buried bones of one of their
sky-cities, hurled from the heavens in their fall. The wind sings strange songs here, and even my
most hardened men feel uneasy in their sleep.
Among the relics retrieved, one draws my particular attention. A perfect cube, six inches across. It is
of stone, but not like any I know. Smooth, cool, unyielding. Runes mark each of its faces, softly
aglow. I cannot read them, though I am no novice to the arcane. Their meaning is utterly alien. I can
feel the power of its magic, like a storm in stillness. And so, I will not risk direct probing—not yet. I
believe the ruins may hold answers, if we can uncover more of the complex buried beneath the
frost.
The locals claim it was brought here by a dying paladin, a man whose name they could not recall.
Only that his shield bore a single yellow rose. Before death took him, he begged the Vistani who
found him to hide the cube away, to protect it from those who would seek it out. That it must
remain buried.
A curious thing. I wonder if this relic connects to that fringe order who dwell now in Valls—the ones
that wear the bright blue robes who Grandfather permitted to settle within the city. They, too,
carry the mark of the Yellow Rose if memory serves.
One of the Vistani who guided us to the site—a woman—was unlike any I’ve encountered. Her
name is Tatyana. She is... radiant. Her emerald eyes seem to hold a thousand years of secrets.
There is a spirit in her, wild and beautiful. I’ve never met her like in any court or campaign. Were it
not for her, we might never have found the ruin at all.
Give my love to Mother. Tell her that Castle Ravenloft grows more magnificent by the week. The
eastern wing is nearly complete, and the central tower now rises high above the valley. By next
Leaffall, it will be ready for your visit.
In glory and in blood,
Strahd Von Zarovich
Prince of Barovia
Lord of the Eastern Marches

Letter from Lord Geoffrey Frostmantle to Mistress Clarissa Allyson
In the Name of the Crown,
Mistress Allyson,
The bearer of this letter comes to you in dire times, under my authority and with my trust. They are
tasked with reaching you on matters of utmost urgency. The shadows that have spread across our
land have deepened, and I fear the darkness has now fully taken hold.
The Prince, my nephew, Ian Frostmantle, is gravely wounded. I am doing what I must to secure his
survival, but I cannot fight this battle on two fronts. You have long been the keeper of knowledge and
wisdom, and now, more than ever, we need that wisdom to understand what has befallen us. The
creatures and mist—this evil—must be tied to something darker than what we had imagined.
I have entrusted these brave souls to deliver this message to you, and I beg that you give them your
guidance. They carry with them the hope of all of Damara. Assist them in any way you can, and
uncover the magic that plagues our land.
I will seek refuge and tend to the Prince, but time is not on our side. The battle we fought was only
the beginning. What you learn may be the difference between the survival or the end of our people.
Trust them as you would trust me. Together, we may still have a chance to turn the tide.
In service to the realm,
Lord Geoffrey Frostmantle
Lord of Westwatch, Protector of the Western Reaches

Letter of Argynvost
A copy preserved in Wachterhaus.
My knights have fallen, and this land is lost. The armies of my enemy will not be stopped by sword or spell, steel or fang. T oday I will die, not avenging
those who have fallen, but defending that which I love—this valley, this home, and the ideals of the Order of the Silver Dragon.
The evil surrounds me. The time has come to throw off this guise and show them the heat of my true form. Let them see me as I truly am. Let them see the
honor and glory that lies in a dragon’s heart. Let them tell their stories of dark triumph against the protector of the Balin ok Mountains. Let Argynvost be
remembered as a beacon of honor and light.
My one regret is that my remaining days are too few to see my beloved land returned to its rightful people. Yet I know that m y sacrifice will not be in vain.
Let them see me, not as their enemy, but as their deliverance. Let them see the truth! Though my mind may forget, my spirit w ill never die. Let it soar as a
beacon of light throughout these darkened lands to bring hope to land wrought with despair.
Now, to battle!
From On the Lower Stones Beneath the Amber Halls
Unsigned. Probably a field paper.
The visible temple walls and the buried walls do not agree in style, purpose, or age. The upper work is stern and holy in its own hard way. The lower work is
technical. That is the only word for it. The angles repeat. The channels repeat. The old sockets in the stone are spaced too evenly to be decorative.
In one chamber below the broken stairs there are circular housings cut into the wall at shoulder height. They are empty now. Three on the left side. Two
on the right. The nearest comparison I have found in any manuscript is a damaged sketch in a Netherese paper.. If that compar ison is right, then the buried
place under the Amber Temple was built by the Netherese.
From the private notes of Nikolai Wachter
Folded into the back of a ledger.
Supper with the Count again. He was in one of those moods where he speaks little at first, then all at once. He said the firs t he ever heard of the buried
halls under the mountain was from Tatyana herself. I thought that only sentiment talking until he fetched three books, two maps, and a rubbing from a
broken stone and laid them all before me.
We sat there for hours after the meal, chasing names through old texts and matching fragments like clerks gone mad. At one po int he found the same old
place-name in two separate sources and struck the table so hard he nearly upset the lamp. I am ashamed to say I did much the same n ot long after.
We looked like fools. Two old men hunched over books, arguing and laughing at scraps of dead language. If anyone had seen us, they’d have taken us for
schoolmasters, not lords.
What Fiona shared with Thorian over dinner about the letter
Fiona was quiet for a while after she read the letter. She folded it once, very carefully, and set it beside her plate. Then she said Nikolai used to speak of
those evenings often, especially in the years before he died. She said Strahd first told Nikolai about the buried halls in th e mountain after speaking of
Tatyana. According to him, she had shown him an old place beneath the high stone years before, something older than the temple and o lder than the
valley as they knew it. That was enough to hook them both. After that, whenever the Count came to supper and the mood took hi m, the meal would go
cold while he and Nikolai dragged out maps, old books, and scraps of copied text.
Fiona said they were ridiculous together when they got going. Two grown men, both too important for the sort of behavior, lea ning over the table and
arguing over names and dates, then lighting up when something finally matched. “Like schoolboys,” she said. “Like two schoolb oys who thought they had
found the world’s best-kept secret.” Then she looked at the letter again and said, more quietly, "that Nikolai had never looked more alive than he did on
those nights."
From On Certain Netherese Outworks Beyond the Empire Proper
Scholarly copy. No author named.
The later Netherese did not keep all their important work in the center of the empire. Some things were sent far out, either for privacy or because the
work itself was thought too dangerous to keep near their great enclaves. Several such sites are named with the word Thul. In the fragments that survive,
the word seems to mark places of study or research. One of these was Thul Vharak, a mountain station where the focus was shielding, warding, and the
holding of dangerous force inside fixed bounds. The name most often tied to the site is Archmage Vael-Toruun.

From A Speculation on Thul Seyr and the Problem of Passage
A later scholar trying to make sense of broken Netherese fragments.
The surviving notes on Thul Seyr are thin, which usually means the work there mattered. Vharak shows up in discussions of shields and containment.
Avenar appears in connection with something called 'The Great Machine' or 'Strong Device'.. Hard to translate without full co ntext... Seyr is different. Its
fragments speak of distance planes, or maybe realities?
One copied line appears twice in unrelated sources: any world (or maybe reality), any time. It may have been a motto for the project.
From A Short Treatise on Mythallar Crystals
Dry, practical writing. Several words underlined
Later collectors call them Ioun Stones and treat them as curiosities. That is not what they were made for. In older Netherese notes they are called
mythallar crystals. The larger mythallar held the power. These smaller crystals took a measured share of it and carried it where it was needed. They were
fitted into engines, ward-lines, moving devices, and constructs. A machine built to receive one would not work properly without it. The craft is lost n ow.
Most people who find one think they’ve found a rare stone.
