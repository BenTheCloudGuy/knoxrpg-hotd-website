# The Netheril Empire

- **Type:** Fallen Civilization / Ancient Magical Empire
- **Base of Operations:** The lands now known as the Great Desert of Anauroch, northern Faerûn
- **Status:** Destroyed — fell in −339 DR during Karsus's Folly
- **Alignment:** Neutral (corrupted from within)

---

## Ancient History

The Empire of Netheril rose in the northern reaches of Faerûn roughly 3,900 years before the Dalereckoning calendar. In the lands that would later become the Great Desert of Anauroch, a region of rivers, forests, lakes, and plains along the coast of the Narrow Sea, a loose group of fishing and farming villages known as the Alliance of Seventon began to grow around −3,859 DR. The settlements of Fenwick, Gers, Gilan, Gustaf, Moran, Nauseef, and Janick made up this early alliance. They were humble people, nothing like the arcane empire they would become.

The elves of Eaerlann, watching the growing human realm with cautious interest, opened diplomatic ties and began instructing the humans in the ways of arcane magic shortly after the Alliance formed. The humans proved astonishingly talented. They learned spells with a speed and hunger the elves had never seen. Following this association, the alliance was renamed "Netheril," meaning Nether's Land, by a leader known as Nether the Elder. Within Netheril, every citizen was taught the very basics of spellcasting. Even common servants knew cantrips to help with daily tasks. Those who truly excelled became known as arcanists, a rapidly rising political class that would eventually dominate the empire.

### The Ages of Netheril

Netheril's history is divided into distinct ages, each marked by a major development in arcane understanding.

**The Nether Age** began roughly 300 years after the elves first taught the Netherese magic. In −3533 DR, an arcanist known as the Finder discovered the Nether Scrolls within the ruins of the fallen elven empire of Aryvandaar. These ancient magical texts contained arcane knowledge far beyond anything the elves had shared. The Nether Scrolls became the foundation of Netherese power and the empire's greatest secret. During this age, a 33-year-old prodigy named Congenio Ioun created the first ioun stone, a feat of magical craftsmanship that had never been achieved before. The arcanists also conquered the Land of Alabaster Towers and stripped away arcane spells from the minds of its sorcerers.

**The Mythallar Era** was ushered in by the birth of Ioulaum in −3315 DR, one of Netheril's greatest arcanists. Ioulaum led the defense against a massive orc horde from the Plain of Standing Stones, then changed the world forever by inventing the first mythallar in −3014 DR. With this device, the Netherese could power quasi-magical items directly from the Weave itself, bypassing the normal limits of spellcasting. In −2954 DR, Ioulaum used the spell Proctiv's move mountain to lift the first floating enclave, Xinlenal, into the sky. During this era, one of the two complete sets of Nether Scrolls was stolen by Cormanthyrian elves aided by a rock gnome named Rilmohx Sha'Quessir, who glimpsed a single scroll and absorbed the entire school of illusion magic into his mind. That knowledge spread through the gnome communities of Faerûn.

**The Silver Age** began with the founding of the empire's ruling council, the High Mages of Netheril, in −2758 DR. The Netherese discovered ore deposits beneath the Greypeak Mountains, established the mines of Dekanter, and founded colonies in the North. Expansion westward was driven by the Terraseer, a mysterious oracle who located the Old Owl Well in −2368 DR, though the outpost secretly served as a way to access a cache of chardalyn, a rare magic-absorbing mineral.

**The Golden Age** lasted roughly a thousand years and was marked by extraordinary expansion. Netheril began lifting one enclave into the sky per year. Dozens of floating cities soon rode the air currents above the empire's borders. During this period, the arcanists established three undersea, crystal-domed colonies known as Deep Netheril beneath the Sea of Fallen Stars. After the Netherese enslaved the aquatic elves of Serôs, the high mages of Aryselmalyr destroyed the colonies in a catastrophic undersea explosion that created the Saerloon and Selgaunt Bays. Jeriah Chronos, born in −2207 DR, founded the school of chronomancy during this era.

**The Age of Discovery** saw the Netherese push beyond Toril itself. In −1205 DR they discovered chardalyn beneath the Plain of Standing Stones, leading to experiments in gem magic. They built flying ships called spelljammers and ventured outside Toril's crystal sphere, where they encountered illithids for the first time. The mind flayers attacked on sight, and the hostility ended Netherese spelljamming efforts for a thousand years.

**The Shadowed Age** was the empire's final era. It began with the birth of Karsus in −696 DR. He was a savant among prodigies, mastering spellcraft almost instantly. He created the enclave of Eileanar, discovered heavy magic, and killed the rogue arcanist Wulgreth, transforming him into a lich. In −553 DR the Netherese discovered the Plane of Shadow, a discovery that would prove fateful. And in −461 DR, the phaerimm, subterranean creatures maddened by the magical residue seeping into the earth from the floating cities, began their war against Netheril, draining life and magic from both Toril and the enclaves, turning fertile lands into barren wastelands and zones of wild magic. Enclaves fell. Arcanists fled. The commoners of Low Netheril, abandoned by the wizards who ruled them, scattered in search of safer lands. In −345 DR, the Terraseer appeared in the enclave of Eileanar with a final warning: Mystryl would challenge Netheril in a manner never before seen.

## Politics and Governance

Netheril was governed by a council of the most powerful archwizards in the empire, often referred to as The Twelve. Though the membership changed over the centuries, these individuals controlled the greatest magical resources and political authority in Netheril. Their rule was unstable by nature. Rival arcanists constantly competed for greater knowledge and power, and political intrigue, magical experimentation, and personal ambition defined Netherese governance at every level.

Magical ability was the ultimate measure of authority. Arcanists possessed the legal right to command lesser citizens, and many enforced their rule through magical constructs and summoned creatures. Three legal principles shaped Netherese society more than any others:

- **Arcane Supremacy:** Arcanists possessed legal authority over non-magical citizens in all matters.
- **Magical Experimentation:** Research was rarely restricted, even when dangerous. Innovation was valued above caution.
- **Enclave Autonomy:** Each floating city governed itself under its ruling archwizard, with the council serving as a loose coordinating body rather than a centralized government.

This system allowed magical innovation to flourish. It also led to the reckless experimentation that would eventually destroy the empire.

## The People of Netheril

Netherese society was rigidly divided between those who wielded arcane power and those who did not. Three classes defined the empire:

**The Arcanists** were powerful wizards who controlled the government, magical research, and the floating enclaves. Their influence was absolute, and many viewed themselves as beings above ordinary mortals. They lived in the sky cities, surrounded by wonders of their own making, and rarely concerned themselves with the lives of those below.

**The High Citizens** were merchants, scholars, priests, and lesser nobles who served the magical elite. Though not all possessed magical talent, they lived in the great cities and enjoyed wealth and influence. They acted as the intermediaries between the arcanists and the common people, and many grew rich from the trade that flowed through the empire's vast network of sky routes and surface roads.

**The Low Netherese** were the farmers, laborers, and craftsmen who supported the empire's vast infrastructure. Most lived in the towns and villages scattered across Netheril's surface lands. They worked the fields, raised the livestock, mined the stone and metal, and produced the food and raw materials that kept the floating cities fed and supplied. The Low Netherese looked up at the enclaves every day and saw a world they would never reach. They were the foundation the empire stood on, and the arcanists barely acknowledged they existed.

Among the Low Netherese were the ancestors of the people who would one day become the Vistani. They were travelers and laborers who moved between settlements, trading goods and carrying news across the empire's surface roads. They had no fixed home and no political power. When Netheril fell, they were among the countless displaced who were driven from their homes with nothing but what they could carry. The traveling that began as survival eventually became the defining feature of their identity, and they called it "the Travel."

## The Floating Cities

The greatest achievement of Netherese magic was the creation of flying cities known as enclaves. At the empire's height, 54 floating cities soared above the skies of Faerûn. Many were built on the peaks of mountains, carved off and inverted, while others were existing surface cities elevated by the empire's arcanists. Using immense magical engines called mythallars, the Netherese lifted these cities into the sky. A mythallar was a colossal magical sphere capable of generating enormous arcane energy. This power sustained the floating cities and allowed Netherese arcanists to cast spells far beyond the normal limits of magic, and to create quasi-magical items that drew power directly from the mythallar rather than the Weave. These cities floated miles above the earth, visible from the ground as glittering jewels in the sky.

The mythallar was not just a power source. It was the central node of a vast magical network. Smaller tuned crystals drew measured power from it and carried that energy into the empire's great works. These crystals powered wards, engines, levitating structures, weapons, and constructs. What later ages call Ioun Stones were, in many cases, the surviving remnants of that older science. Every true automaton carried such a crystal at its heart. It was more than a battery. It regulated power, held arcane instructions, and linked the construct to the greater magical systems around it. The Netherese called these cores mythallar crystals. The knowledge required to make them died with Netheril, and now the world mistakes their surviving creations for relics or curiosities, never realizing they were once pieces of a much larger design.

The enclaves were by and large bastions of enlightenment, peace, and ingenuity, giving the mages sanctuaries from which they could pursue arcane research safe from the distractions of the surface world. The nobility of High Netheril spoke Loross, a language written in the same script as Draconic. Their influence was bounded only by the other empires of the age: Illefarn and Calimshan in the west, Cormanthyr and Jhaamdath around the Inner Sea.

Among the most notable enclaves:

- **Xinlenal** — The first of all enclaves, raised by Ioulaum in −2954 DR. A center of magical research and experimentation that paved the way for every floating city that followed.
- **Thultanthar** — The City of Shade, led by arcanist Telamont Tanthul, who conducted significant research on the Shadow Weave. Days before Karsus's Folly, Tanthul shifted the entire enclave to the Plane of Shadow. The city survived the crash that killed the other enclaves, but was trapped in a shadowed realm with no way back. Over the centuries, the influence of Shar and the Shadowfell gradually corrupted its people into shades known as the Shadovar.
- **Eileanar** — One of the last enclaves created, home of Karsus himself.
- **Sakkors** — A military stronghold formed from an inverted mountaintop, whose arcanist Xolund imbued his own sentience into the city's mythallar.
- **Anauria** — A wealthy enclave known for its grand libraries and academies. One of only three enclaves saved from destruction during the Fall by the reborn goddess Mystra.
- **Akintaer** — An enclave with a mighty army devoted to the war god Targus.
- **Spiel** — A center of enlightenment with a great number of universities and places of learning.
- **Synod** — A sacerdotal sanctuary dedicated to Amaunator, Mystryl, and Tyche.
- **Orbedal** — Called Sanctuary, a haven of peace and serenity for the Netherese nobility.
- **Jiksidur** — Ruled by the sorcerer-king Larloch, who stood guard against the battlemages of Raumathar.
- **Ythryn** — An enclave whose mages searched for Ostoria, the ancient giant kingdom. It fell over Icewind Dale into the Reghed Glacier.
- **Negarath** — Whose remnants would go on to form the kingdom of Barze in the realm that became Vaasa.

### Low Netheril

Below the floating cities, the lands of Low Netheril consisted of scattered towns, land-bound settlements known as demesnes, and roaming barbarian tribes. The demesnes were ruled by lesser arcanists who had not yet mastered the creation of mythallars. Each demesne could exhibit its own terrain and weather, alterable at the whim of its ruling arcanist.

The commoners of Low Netheril, also called the free folk, were seen as chattel by their societal superiors. The arcanists used them for labor and occasionally as subjects for magical experimentation. Heavily influenced by the priests of the Netherese pantheon, the serfs looked to their gods for a life free from the subjugation of the empire's magocratic rulers. The fringes of the empire were inhabited by the roaming Rengarth and Angardt barbarian tribes.

Notable Low Netherese settlements included the Mines of Dekanter, which produced gold, iron, mercury, silver, and platinum; Hlaungadath on the edge of the High Ice; Rasilith, capital of the Land of Alabaster Towers; and the colony at Old Owl Well, built to spy on the elves of Illefarn but secretly serving as a waypoint to a chardalyn cache.

The division between the sky cities and the surface grew extreme over time. The arcanists in their floating enclaves became increasingly isolated from the lives of ordinary people. They forgot, or chose to ignore, that everything they had was built on the labor of those below.

## Karsus's Folly and the Fall

The greatest and most dangerous archwizard Netheril ever produced was Karsus. A prodigy whose magical skill surpassed that of his peers, Karsus believed the gods themselves had become a limitation on mortal progress. As Netheril faced growing threats from the phaerimm, ancient and malevolent beings of immense magical power expanding through the Underdark, Karsus sought a solution that would ensure the survival of his empire forever.

In −339 DR, Karsus completed the most powerful spell ever created: Karsus's Avatar. The spell was designed to steal the divine power of a god, allowing Karsus to become a deity himself. His target was Mystryl, the goddess who governed the Weave of magic. When the spell succeeded, the consequences were catastrophic. For a brief moment Karsus became a god, but he could not control the immense power he had seized. The strain shattered the Weave itself.

Mystryl sacrificed herself to restore magic to the world, but the damage was already done. When magic failed, the floating enclaves of Netheril immediately lost the power that kept them aloft. One by one the great cities of the sky fell from the heavens. Entire populations were destroyed as their cities crashed into the earth. High Netheril was ruined in a matter of hours.

Almost immediately after Mystryl died, she was reincarnated as Mystra, who retained her position as goddess of magic. Despite the enormity of the destruction, Mystra managed to save three of the falling enclaves, gently bringing them to earth: Anauria, Asram, and Hlondath. The lives of their noble citizens were spared, and each became the seed of a successor state. But the rest fell, and their ruins were scattered across northern Faerûn, their residual magic influencing the region for centuries.

## The Chaos That Followed

The Fall did not end with the cities hitting the ground. The destruction of the Weave sent shockwaves through every magical system the Netherese had built. Wild magic surged across the region in unpredictable storms. Spells misfired or triggered without warning. Magical constructs went haywire. Wards that had stood for centuries collapsed, releasing whatever they had been containing.

The phaerimm, the subterranean creatures whose war with Netheril had already weakened the empire, continued their life-draining magic after the Fall. Their spells slowly turned the once fertile lands of Netheril into the vast desert now known as Anauroch. The phaerimm were eventually defeated and trapped behind a shimmering barrier called the sharn wall by the mysterious aberrant sharn, but the damage was done. The desert remained.

Because of what came to be called Mystra's Ban, the mythallars could no longer draw upon the Weave to power quasi-magical items or to create new floating enclaves. The extremely powerful spells that Netherese arcanists had wielded were no longer granted to mortal practitioners. The goddess of Magic deemed such power beyond mortal responsibility. Epic magic was thereafter limited to practitioners of Elven High Magic.

Thultanthar, the City of Shade, escaped the physical crash by shifting into the Plane of Shadow days before the Folly. When Telamont Tanthul saw the catastrophe that had occurred in his absence, he assumed the phaerimm had caused the destruction and decided to remain in the Shadow Plane, hoping to preserve what might be the last of the magically gifted Netherese. He vowed to one day return and rebuild the Empire of Magic. The clerics of Shar within the enclave quickly divined the truth of what had happened but shared it only with the nobility. Over the centuries, the influence of the Shadowfell corrupted the Netherese of Thultanthar into shades, and their enclave became known as the City of Shade.

Kingdoms fractured. Armies scattered. Entire civilizations were swallowed by war, famine, and the unchecked release of wild magic. Whole regions became uninhabitable overnight. The world burned, and the people who survived had to keep moving or die.

## Survivors and Successor States

### The Successor States

The three enclaves saved by Mystra each became the foundation of a new realm, though none would last.

**Anauria** settled in the lands directly south of the fallen empire. The Anaurians became renowned for crafting fine swords and carrying on the magical legacy of Netheril. Without mythallars or high-level magic, their efforts were severely limited. The kingdom was finally destroyed in 450 DR in a bloody and costly battle against an orc horde.

**Asram** barely had time to recover from the Fall before it was struck by a plague sent by Talona, the goddess of disease and poison, in −33 DR. Cattle died in the fields, crops withered overnight, and within the first week of summer the plague claimed the entire nation. Almost no one survived.

**Hlondath** was the longest-lived of the successor states. Its people refused to accept that Karsus had caused the Fall, instead blaming the elves of Cormanthyr as "accomplices of the phaerimm." They established good relations with the dwarves of Tethyamar but otherwise concentrated on logging and cattle herding. In 199 DR, the Hlondathans provoked the elves of Myth Drannor into a war known as the Crown Against Scepter Wars. Hlondath was repeatedly crushed in battle and the kingdom was greatly weakened. As the Anauroch desert slowly spread across their lands, the remaining Netherese survivors scattered across Faerûn.

The commoners of Low Netheril fragmented into primitive tribes. Many settled in the North, developing into the Angardt and Rengarth barbarian tribes or intermingling with the people who became the Uthgardt.

### The Vistani

Among the Low Netherese who survived the Fall were the people who would become the Vistani. They were travelers and laborers, displaced with nothing but what they could carry. They moved from ruin to ruin, scavenging food and resources as nations tore each other apart in the chaos that followed. They learned to read the land, to find safe passage where others saw only danger, and to keep moving when the ground beneath them turned hostile. Over time, the traveling itself became something more than survival. It became who they were, and they called it "the Travel."

The Vistani eventually made their way to the Valley of Barovia, where they established encampments and built a life in the quiet valley tucked between the Balinok Mountains. They never stopped traveling. The Travel was too deeply woven into their identity to abandon. Barovia was their anchor, but it was never a cage.

### Halruaa

Far to the south, an arcanist named Raumark led a group of refugees in their airships to the Shining South. They eventually reached the remote region of Halruaa, deep in South Faerûn, well away from Netheril's old center and the wild magic storms that consumed the north. Other survivors were guided through subterranean passages by the dwarves of Ascore to the realm of Delzoun.

In Halruaa, these survivors founded a new magical society built on the remnants of Netherese knowledge. Unlike their predecessors, the Halruaans learned from Netheril's mistakes. They studied the old magic carefully and with greater restraint, preserving the empire's arcane traditions without repeating its worst excesses. Halruaa became one of the most magically advanced nations in Faerûn, though it never approached the raw power or the reckless ambition of the empire that had given it birth.

## Legacy

The centuries of arcane research in Netheril gave Toril a variety of never-before-seen magical items that continued to be created and used by wizards and adventurers for millennia, including Ioun stones. A small number of powerful artifacts were left behind within the ruins of the fallen enclaves, including archyre crowns, chardalyn gems, and shatter-rings. Some quasi-magical items still functioned, but only within a certain distance of the old Low Netherese demesnes where residual mythallar energy persisted.

Netherese ruins were scattered across all of Toril, buried under desert sands, mountain ranges, glaciers, and even the sea floor. Many of these sites still contained artifacts of immense power, some poorly understood by modern scholars. The residual magic and power from the fallen enclaves would influence the Realms for centuries, drawing treasure seekers, cultists, and the desperate to places they had no business entering.

## DM Notes

**This entire section is DM-only. Players should not have access to this information. It can be revealed through gameplay as the campaign progresses.**

### The Corruption of Shorthagot

The destruction of the floating cities was only the most visible consequence of Netheril's fall. The empire's true rot went far deeper, and it started long before Karsus ever cast his spell.

During the height of Netheril's power, arcanists began experimenting with forces believed to exist beyond the Weave of magic itself. They called it Primordial Aether, a cosmic power believed to predate both the gods and the Weave. These experiments attracted the attention of something ancient and terrible.

Shorthagot, the avatar of the Chained God Tharizdun, had survived the Dawn War and continued to act in his master's name for millennia, seeking ways to weaken the divine prison that held Tharizdun in the Abyss. When the Netherese began probing the boundaries of reality, Shorthagot saw an opportunity.

### Vael-Toruun: Shorthagot's Alias in Netheril

Shorthagot infiltrated Netheril's highest circles by taking the identity of **Archmage Vael-Toruun, Master of Wards and Engines**. Publicly, Vael-Toruun was known as one of Netheril's greatest arcane engineers, a brilliant builder of magical systems, defensive latticeworks, containment engines, and power-distribution networks. His seal on a project meant the Empire considered it vital.

Vael-Toruun was not tied to a single site. He oversaw multiple state-level arcane projects across Netheril, including ward architecture, mythallar energy routing, arcane containment fields, construct cores, planar barrier studies, and deep-site magical research facilities. This access was precisely the point. As Master of Wards and Engines, Shorthagot had authority over everything he needed to guide the empire's greatest minds toward building the one thing that could free his master: the Great Machine.

Under Vael-Toruun's influence, the Netherese began constructing a vast arcane superstructure designed to channel Primordial Aether through a network of dimensional anchors and magical conduits. The Netherese believed it could provide limitless power, greater even than a mythallar. But the Machine had another purpose entirely. Under Shorthagot's quiet direction, the Netherese altered the Machine so that it would not merely channel Primordial Aether but open a gateway through which Shorthagot himself could enter the world fully, and through which Tharizdun's chains could be weakened.

### The Thuls: Netherese Research Sites

To build the Great Machine, Vael-Toruun established a network of secret research sites across the empire, each dedicated to a different aspect of the project. The Netherese called these facilities "Thuls," places of research or study. Each site was chosen for its isolation and its specific advantages.

#### Thul Avenar — "The Gate of the Horizon"
- **Purpose:** Final integration and assembly of the Great Machine
- **Location:** A lost ruin in the far eastern marches of old Netheril, built in a wind-blasted gorge beside the Tur-Pharael, the River of Ghosts
- **What happened there:** This was the main site where all discoveries from the satellite facilities were brought together. The Machine was assembled here: mythallar crystal integration, engine housing and core alignment, final testing of combined systems, containment protocols from Vharak's research, and transit architecture from Seyr's research. In old records, it would have looked like an imperial research citadel. In truth, it was the central body of the whole project.
- **Key figure:** Melliar Thond, Master of Lore and Records, was the last witness of Thul Avenar. His account, "Reflections Before the Fall," describes the Machine's activation and its terrible consequences. He called it the Infernal Machine.
- **Present day:** Buried beneath collapsed cavern systems after the magical backlash of the Fall. The Order of the Yellow Rose was later founded atop its ruins.

#### Thul Vharak — "The Bastion"
- **Purpose:** Arcane shields, warding, containment, defensive latticework
- **Location:** Beneath Mount Ghakis, along the Ghakis-Giantspire range in the eastern Damaran-Barovian arc
- **What happened there:** Shield arrays, anti-siege ward theory, layered containment fields, power redirection through crystal matrices, and the prison architecture later used to bind Shorthagot. This is where the Netherese learned how to hold power, and the site that best explains the later role of the Order of the Yellow Rose. When Netheril collapsed, this site could be hidden, sealed, and remembered only by oath.

#### Thul Seyr — "The Breach"
- **Purpose:** Veil-piercing, planar contact, transport, breach theory
- **Location:** A dead and buried site on the eastern edge of the Plains of Purple Dust, far from casual traffic
- **What happened there:** Communication across planes, gate theory, realm-to-realm transit, spatial folding, temporal echo studies, and breach stabilization. This is where Netheril stopped asking "Can we speak across worlds?" and started asking "Can we force reality open?"

#### Thul Mystren — "The Crucible"
- **Purpose:** Mythallar crystal refinement and power-routing theory
- **Location:** Hidden in the mountains north of the Shining Plains, far from Netheril proper but connected by old sky routes
- **What happened there:** Refinement of mythallar crystals, calibration of Ioun-stone taps, arcane load distribution, automaton core housing, and failure testing for unstable crystal resonance. This is where raw theory became usable crystal architecture. At the table, this is the site to use when players learn that Ioun Stones were not ornaments. They were machine parts.
- **Present-day rumor:** Miners sometimes find smooth crystal fragments that hum when brought near old magic.

#### Thul Karath — "The Forge of Minds"
- **Purpose:** Construct intelligence, automatons, command lattices
- **Location:** Buried under the broken uplands west of the Rawlinswood, on a forgotten plateau that once had direct sky access
- **What happened there:** Automaton cores, command sigils, obedience lattices, crystal memory matrices, war-construct prototypes, and integrated shielded chassis using Vharak data. If Thul Mystren made the crystals, Thul Karath taught them how to turn those crystals into minds.
- **Present-day result:** Dead constructs, half-buried in stone, with chest cavities empty where their cores were removed centuries ago.

#### Thul Oraph — "The Far Eye"
- **Purpose:** Long-range observation, divinatory mapping, remote sensing across realms
- **Location:** In the high, hidden reaches of the Halruaan northern walls, far to the south where no one would think to look
- **What happened there:** Remote arcane observation, target acquisition for planar openings, divinatory triangulation, and location fixing across worlds. This is where the Netherese learned how to see.

### The Great Machine and the Fall

When the Weave collapsed during Karsus's Folly, the arcane network sustaining the Great Machine destabilized. The catastrophic surge of magical energy nearly tore open the gateway the Netherese had been constructing under Shorthagot's direction. In the chaos that followed, the remaining researchers realized the horror they had nearly unleashed.

Using the last remnants of their High Netherese rituals, they performed a desperate containment working. The Machine was shattered into multiple arcane components, and its power was sealed behind dimensional locks. Shorthagot, revealed at last for what he was, was sealed within the Great Machine itself by the champions who fought him. The corrupted research complex of Thul Avenar was buried beneath the mountains as the magical backlash collapsed entire cavern systems.

The magic binding Shorthagot was divided into four powerful relics, later known as the Keys of Shorthagot. Each key was entrusted to one of the champions and hidden in distant lands. One was carried east into the mountains of Damara by Saint Aurellan Dawnward, who founded the Order of the Yellow Rose to guard it. The few surviving Netherese texts spoke only in fragmented warnings: *"Let the Machine remain silent, lest the Sleeper awaken."*

When Vael-Toruun was believed dead along with countless other archmages after the Fall, the truth was buried. He was not destroyed. He was sealed within the Great Machine he helped create, and the knowledge of that prison was hidden by the first Order of the Yellow Rose. The four sacred keys were set apart to keep the prison closed, and the order was founded to guard that secret until the end of ages.
