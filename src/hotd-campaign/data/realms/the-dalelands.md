# The Dalelands

![Map: Heartlands](../../images/realms/map-02.006-heartlands.jpg)

> **At a Glance:** These are lands of chivalrous knights, dastardly despots, rich and scheming merchants, and backwoods farmers just trying to make a living.
> **Region:** Heartlands
> **Languages:** Chondathan, Damaran

The Dalelands is a loose collection of tiny vales called dales, each with its own territory, governance, and character. The region is known for its independent spirit and the diversity of its inhabitants, ranging from humans and elves to halflings and dwarves. The dales are nestled around the expansive forest of Cormanthor, which has historically been a significant influence on the region's culture and politics. Each dale operates autonomously, with its own local government, but they share a common cultural heritage and often band together for mutual defense. The governance of the Dalelands is characterized by a council system, where representatives from each dale gather annually at the Dales Council to address common issues and forge alliances. This decentralized system allows for a great deal of local autonomy, but also requires cooperation and diplomacy among the dales.

The geography of the Dalelands is varied, featuring lush forests, fertile farmland, and rugged hills. The climate is temperate, with distinct seasons that influence the agricultural cycles of the region. Historically, the Dalelands have been a battleground for larger powers like Cormyr and Sembia, but the dales have maintained their independence through a combination of diplomacy and military alliances. The culture of the Dalelands is a blend of human and elven traditions, with a strong emphasis on community, self-reliance, and respect for the natural world.

## Notable Locations

### Dales Compact

When human settlers first arrived in the area now known as the Dalelands, the forest of Cormanthor was already occupied by a thriving elven civilization. These wary elves proposed the Dales Compact: an agreement that allowed humans to settle on the edges of Cormanthor or in places that the great trees didn’t grow, so long as they didn’t cut deeper into the forest. Human mages erected a stone obelisk called the Standing Stone in Cormanthor as a symbol of unity between the two species. This date became the beginning of the Dalereckoning, or 1 DR, marking the first year of the modern Faerunian calendar. Though Cormanthor’s elven civilization fell long ago and few elves still live there, the people of the Dalelands still respect the Dales Compact.

### Archenbridge

A history of Sembian invasions has led the trading hub of Archenbridge (the chief settlement of Archendale) to become the most fortified town in the Dalelands. Most goods from Cormyr and Sembia flow through this walled, trap-filled town to the rest of the dales. The town is known for its strategic military importance and its bustling markets, where merchants from across the Heartlands come to trade. 

### Cormanthor

Cormanthor is a forest of towering trees. Most of the elves who once lived here left long ago, but some refugees of Myth Drannor’s second fall remain. A swath of fungi, oozes, and rot called Moander’s Road was carved through Cormanthor’s northern reaches by the rampaging god of corruption and rot, Moander. The forest is also home to various magical creatures and ancient ruins, making it a place of both wonder and danger.

### Myth Drannor

The elven metropolis of Myth Drannor fell hundreds of years ago to demonic forces. Elves briefly reclaimed it in the last century, but then the flying Shadovar city of Thultanthar crashed into it, doing extensive damage. Now demons and other monsters again infest Myth Drannor’s remains. Its many ruins invite adventurers to seek the lost city’s treasures or find a way to restore its mythal. The city was once a center of magical learning and culture, and its ruins still hold many secrets and powerful artifacts.

### Scardale Town

The infamously lawless harbor of Scardale Town is home to several criminal syndicates and an enclave of Red Wizards. Its people still reel from the memory of the Shaking Plague, a magical contagion that decimated its population. The town is governed by Lord Kharu Aumersair, a tiefling sorcerer who claims descent from Elminster, through the infamous Zhentarim agent Scyllua Darkhope and her father, Lashan Aumersair. Scardale has a history of being ruled by invaders, and Lord Aumersair has used this resentment of outsiders to unite the town’s criminal syndicates into an alliance that supports his rule.

### Shadowdale Town

Though small and unassuming at first glance, many consider sleepy Shadowdale Town the Dalelands’ cultural heart. It was once home to Elminster and Storm Silverhand, though neither has been seen in town for years. These days, a growing community of orcs from the River Ashaba have settled in Shadowdale. Lady Khara Sulwood rules from the twisted Tower of Ashaba, with the help of the elf warrior Tharinel, her reliable bodyguard and adviser. The town is known for its rich history and its role in many significant events in Faerun's history.

### Mistledale

Mistledale is one of the more prosperous dales, known for its fertile farmland and peaceful villages. It is a large, open dale that stretches along the Moonsea Ride, a major trade route. The dale is protected by the Riders of Mistledale, a well-trained militia that keeps the peace and defends against external threats. The capital, Ashabenford, is a bustling market town where farmers, traders, and adventurers gather.

### Deepingdale

Deepingdale is a forested dale known for its close ties to the elven community. It is a place where humans and elves live in harmony, sharing their cultures and traditions. The dale is governed by the Council of Leaves, a group of representatives from both communities. The town of Highmoon serves as the capital and is known for its beautiful architecture and vibrant cultural festivals.

### Harrowdale

Harrowdale, the oldest of the dales, is located on the eastern edge of the Dalelands. It is a coastal dale with a strong maritime tradition, and its people are known for their seafaring skills. The town of Harrowdale Town is the main settlement, and it serves as a hub for trade and commerce. The dale has a long history of resilience against invasions and has maintained its independence through strategic alliances and a strong navy.

## DM Notes

The Quivering Forest's connection to Barovia, mentioned in the Moonsea section of FRHoF, is significant. Though the mists have cleared, creatures from Barovia still haunt those woods. This represents one of the points where Strahd's domain has touched the Material Plane, and it may be relevant if the party seeks information about Barovia from the outside world.
