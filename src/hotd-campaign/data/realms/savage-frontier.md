# Savage Frontier

![Map: The North](../../images/realms/map-02.008-the-north.jpg)

> **At a Glance:** In this harsh and beautiful wilderness, nothing lasts and survival is a day-to-day concern.
> **Region:** The North
> **Languages:** Bothii, Chondathan, Illuskan, Reghedjic

The Savage Frontier is a vast, untamed expanse in the North of Faerun. Characterized by its rugged terrain, dense forests, and towering mountains, the region is largely ungoverned and inhabited by a myriad of dangerous creatures. The climate is harsh, with long, cold winters and brief, mild summers. The human settlements that do exist are isolated and often under threat from the wild lands that surround them. Historically, the area has been a battleground for various factions, including orc tribes, giant clans, and ambitious adventurers seeking to carve out their own domains. Despite its dangers, the Savage Frontier is rich in resources, attracting those willing to brave its perils for the promise of wealth and glory.

## Notable Locations

### Evermoors

An unsettled region of fog-shrouded hills, cold bogs, rocky ridges, small peaks, and crumbling ruins, this vast waste is inhabited by hill giants, ettins, ogres, countless trolls, and warring bands of orcs. A mile above the Evermoors floats the cloud giant castle of Lyn Armaal, only fleetingly glimpsed through overcast skies. The Evermoors are notorious for their treacherous terrain and the frequent conflicts between the monstrous inhabitants, making it a perilous area for travelers and adventurers.

### Glimmerwood

This wide forest combines the dense, lycanthrope-prowled Moonwood, the Druarwood (claimed by Uthgardt tribes), and the Cold Wood, which is covered by ice and snow even during the height of summer, except where blackened by traveling fire giants. The Glimmerwood is a place of ancient magic and mystery, with hidden enclaves of fey creatures and ancient ruins that hint at long-lost civilizations. The Uthgardt tribes consider parts of the forest sacred and fiercely defend their territories against intruders.

### High Forest

The greatest existing forest in Faerun is vast and mysterious, a remnant from ages past, when elven realms like Illefarn and Eaerlann ruled a continent covered in green. The High Forest is as large as a realm, covering mountains and countless secrets. Here dwell treants of enormous size, stags with antlers wide as a wagon, gigantic bears, owlbears, wolves, centaurs, unicorns, and other creatures stranger still, some of which have no name remembered by mortals. The forest's outermost fringes are inhabited by woodcutters and outlaws, but deeper in, wood elves and Uthgardt warriors of the Ghost Tree tribe guard the forest's secrets. The High Forest is also home to the Star Mounts, a range of mountains that are said to be the resting place of ancient dragons.

### Icespire Peak

Icespire Peak is the tallest of the Sword Mountains, visible from far away as Phandalin. The mountain conceals mithral mines dug by dwarves long ago, and in winter its waterfalls freeze, forming amazing curtains of ice. But Icespire Peak is now home to goblins, frost giants, trolls, the white dragon Cryovain, and agents of the Cult of the Dragon, ensuring all but brave adventurers avoid its perilous slopes. The peak's harsh environment and dangerous inhabitants make it a challenging destination for those seeking the treasures rumored to be hidden within its depths.

### Mere of Dead Men

Thousands of dwarf, elf, human, and orc corpses lie beneath this saltwater swamp, which was created many centuries ago through fell magic. The swamp is home to two ancient black dragons: Voaraghamanthar the Black Death and his twin brother Waervaerendor the Rapacious Raider. The two dragons share a psychic link and are indistinguishable, so they're often mistaken for a single dragon. Efforts to drain the swamp or redirect the High Road away from its borders have been fruitless; the swamp merely expands to fill any abandoned space. The Mere is a place of dark magic and danger, where undead creatures and other horrors lurk beneath the murky waters.

### Phandalin

The frontier settlement of Phandalin has a long history of association with mages and artificers working in the nearby complex of Wave Echo Cave, home to the legendary Forge of Spells. Fragments of Netherese civilization also dot this region, including pieces of psychic crystal. Mind flayers lairing in the Underdark beneath Phandalin collect these crystals and use them to mutate innocent victims in an elaborate scheme to create a new empire in Faerun. Phandalin serves as a base for adventurers exploring the surrounding wilderness and as a hub for trade in the region.

### Icewind Dale

Located in the far northern reaches of the Savage Frontier, Icewind Dale is a frigid, inhospitable land bordered by the Spine of the World mountains. It is home to the Ten Towns, a loose confederation of settlements that rely on fishing and trade to survive. The dale is frequently beset by harsh weather, dangerous wildlife, and marauding orc tribes. Despite these challenges, the people of Icewind Dale are hardy and resilient, carving out a life in one of the most unforgiving environments in Faerun.

### Spine of the World

This massive mountain range forms the northern boundary of the Savage Frontier, separating it from the icy tundra beyond. The Spine of the World is home to numerous tribes of orcs, giants, and other creatures that thrive in its harsh conditions. The mountains are also rich in minerals and precious gems, attracting dwarven miners and adventurers seeking fortune. The range is treacherous, with frequent avalanches and blizzards posing constant threats to those who dare to traverse its peaks.

### Waterdeep

Though not within the Savage Frontier itself, Waterdeep serves as a crucial gateway to the region. Known as the City of Splendors, Waterdeep is a bustling metropolis and one of the most important trade hubs in Faerun. It is a place where adventurers can find supplies, gather information, and hire companions before venturing into the wilds of the Savage Frontier. The city's influence extends far beyond its walls, with many expeditions into the frontier originating from its docks and taverns.
