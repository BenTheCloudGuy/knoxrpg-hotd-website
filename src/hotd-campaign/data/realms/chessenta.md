# Chessenta

![Map: Old Empires](../../images/realms/map-02.009-old-empires.jpg)

> **At a Glance:** Living deities walk among mortals, leading their followers into cycles of terrible wars followed by golden ages.
> **Region:** Old Empires
> **Languages:** Chessentan, Mulhorandi, Untheric

Chessenta is an unusual empire, where a chaotic array of city-states are in perpetual conflict with one another. But where constant strife might weaken lesser realms, Chessenta’s endless saber rattling and infighting only tempers an already resilient people. The region is known for its fierce independence, with each city-state governed by its own ruler or council, leading to a patchwork of alliances and rivalries. The geography of Chessenta is varied, with fertile plains, rugged coastlines, and the imposing Akanapeaks to the north. The climate is generally warm, with hot summers that are ideal for the region's famed vineyards.

Chessentans revel in war the way other cultures delight in sports or feasting. In Chessenta, a youth’s first brush with death is a rite of passage, and a life without battle is no life at all. Chessentans who don’t take part in their city’s bloody military campaigns toil at home to supply the army, growing drought-resistant crops in the thin and stony soil, forging sturdy but lightweight weapons and shields suitable for the hot summer wars, and producing thousands of barrels of the region’s famous ruby wine to keep soldiers’ spirits high. The culture is steeped in martial traditions, with a strong emphasis on personal honor and bravery.

Religion is important in Chessenta, and Chessentans have an array of gods and demigods utterly alien to outsiders. But perhaps no deity in Chessenta is so noteworthy as Tchazzar, a legendary god-king who in antiquity united the city-states of Chessenta and spearheaded the realm’s only golden age. Tchazzar’s death four hundred years ago heralded a great schism between the Chessentan cities. Only recently has the violent deity returned to Faerunian soil in his true form as the Great Red Dragon. After reclaiming and subjugating the city of Cimbar, Tchazzar now aims to reunite the fractious powers of Chessenta into his idealized warrior realm. The return of Tchazzar has sparked both hope and fear among the populace, as his rule promises both unity and the potential for devastating conflict.

## Notable Locations

### Airspur

The city of Airspur drifts above the shores of Wizards’ Reach on an island of floating stone. Its populace includes a higher-than-average percentage of orcs and genasi. The theocratic rulers, the Church of Bhaelros, demand monthly sacrifices to appease their bloody god. The flying city uses its aerial advantage to harry its longstanding political rival, Cimbar, to the east. If not for Cimbar’s divine ruler, Tchazzar, Airspur likely would’ve already destroyed its age-old foe. Airspur is also known for its magical academies that attract students from across Faerun, eager to learn the secrets of elemental magic.

### Cimbar

The former capital of united Chessenta, Cimbar remains one of the fragmented empire’s largest metropolises. The city is Tchazzar’s seat of power. Naturally, given Tchazzar’s draconic nature, the Cult of the Dragon has a strong foothold here. Though it is a blood-soaked and perilous port of call, Cimbar boasts one of the largest navies in the whole Inner Sea region. It also hosts famous non-military institutions such as the University, the greatest center of higher learning in the Old Empires. The city is a hub of trade and culture, with bustling markets and grand theaters that showcase Chessentan drama and music.

### Luthcheq

Military zeal combines with religious fervor in Luthcheq, whose ruling Karanok family persecutes practitioners of arcane magic in the name of Entropy, a strange god of alien intellect that resides somewhere in the vast underground chasms beneath Luthcheq’s undercity. Dozens, if not hundreds, of magically gifted refugees flee Luthcheq each year to escape this destructive cult. The city is heavily fortified, with imposing walls and a large standing army ready to defend against both external threats and internal dissent. Luthcheq's economy is bolstered by its rich deposits of iron and other minerals, which are mined from the nearby mountains.

### Threskel

South of the dormant volcano known as Mount Thulbane and north of the Riders to the Sky lies the sparsely populated land of Threskel. Chessenta and Unther both claim Threskel, but this remote land is famously lorded over by evil dragons. The few people who live in Threskel eke out pitiful existences on barren farms nestled between rocky shores and windswept cliffs. The desperation of this land has given rise to many heroic adventurers whom the dragons eagerly squash. Threskel's harsh environment and the constant threat of dragon attacks make it a land of hardy survivors and bold adventurers.

### Akanapeaks

The Akanapeaks form a natural barrier to the north of Chessenta, separating it from the lands of Thay and the Great Dale. These rugged mountains are home to various tribes and creatures, including giants and griffons. The peaks are rich in minerals, and several mining operations are scattered throughout the range. The Akanapeaks are also a source of adventure, with ancient ruins and hidden valleys waiting to be explored by brave souls.

### Mourktar

Located on the coast of the Alamber Sea, Mourktar is a bustling port city known for its shipyards and mercantile prowess. The city is ruled by a council of merchants who prioritize trade and commerce above all else. Mourktar's markets are filled with goods from across the Inner Sea, and its docks are always busy with ships loading and unloading cargo. The city is also a center for worship of the god Hoar, the Doombringer, whose temple is one of the largest in the region.
